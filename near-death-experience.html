    How often near-death experiences occur, how they score on standard scales, and what EEG records show are ◆ Science. The interpretation that they show consciousness survives death is ✦ Faith or ◇ Philosophy; so is the interpretation that they are purely brain-generated, unless a specific mechanism is tested. Both remain open.

    The prospective studies

    ◆ Science van Lommel et al. 2001. Of 344 resuscitated cardiac patients in 10 Dutch hospitals, 62 (18%) reported a near-death experience and 41 (12%) a core experience. The experience was not associated with the duration of cardiac arrest, unconsciousness, or medication; the authors argue that a purely anoxic explanation would predict more reports (The Lancet, PMID 11755611; DOI). Commentary and letters followed in the journal.

    ◆ Science AWARE (Parnia et al. 2014). Across 2,060 cardiac arrests, 140 survivors were interviewed at stage 1 and 101 at stage 2. 46% had memories, 9% had experiences compatible with a near-death experience, and 2% explicitly recalled seeing or hearing events; one period of awareness could be verified (Resuscitation, PMID 25301715; DOI).

    ◆ Science AWARE II (Parnia et al. 2023). Of 567 in-hospital arrests, 53 survived and 28 were interviewed; 11 (39.3%) reported memories or perceptions, including a recalled experience of death in 6 of 28 (21.4%). No one identified the visual target and one identified the auditory target. EEG activity of normal appearance was seen up to 35–60 minutes into CPR (Resuscitation, PMID 37423492; DOI).

    ◆ Science Experiences are measured with the Greyson NDE Scale (Greyson 1983, Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease) and the newer NDE-C scale (2020).

    Explanations and replies

    ◆ Science Mobbs and Watt review neuroscientific explanations of near-death experiences (Trends in Cognitive Sciences, 2011); Greyson and colleagues replied (2012).

    ◆ Science In rats, Borjigin and colleagues recorded a surge of gamma-band coherence and connectivity after cardiac arrest, interpreted as a possible neural basis of heightened awareness at death (PNAS, 2013). This is an animal model, and there is no evidence that it matches the content of reported experiences.

    Core concept · home chapters 05 and 06

    Immortality

    Definition. Continued existence of the person, or something essential to it, beyond bodily death: as bodily resurrection, a naturally immortal soul, rebirth or liberation, or technological continuity.

    ✦ Faith

    Bodily resurrection (1 Cor 15; resurrection of the dead); the immortality of the soul in Christian tradition (Aquinas, Summa Theologiae I q.75; Catechism ¶366); reincarnation and liberation (moksha); Buddhist rebirth without a self (SEP: Buddha); Daoist soul-aspects (Chapter 01).

    ◇ Philosophy

    The arguments for and against survival, and the puzzles of personal identity, are set out on Chapter 06 (SEP: Afterlife).

    ◆ Science

    No experiment establishes or excludes survival of death. The relevant data are the near-death studies above, the EEG findings of AWARE II, and the neuroscience of identity and memory.

    The worth of a single life

    ◇ Philosophy Treating a single threatened life as “a universe” asserts that human worth is not interchangeable, rather than measuring it by utility.

    ✦ Faith Hans Calmeyer’s self-chosen epitaph in Osnabrück, “as candles glow, only so long… the note still sounds that in him longed a Song to be,” bridges earthly mortality and enduring moral resonance (hanscalmeyer.com).
