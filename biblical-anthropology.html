    A lexicon of the soul

    Each tradition names the soul in its own words. Read side by side, the terms show how differently the human person has been understood.

    Terms for the soul across traditions

    New Testament Greekpsyche (soul, life); pneuma (spirit); soma (body)Resurrection of the body, not only survival of the soul (1 Cor 15).✦ Faith

    ✦ Faith In Catholic teaching the soul is created directly by God and is immortal (Catechism of the Catholic Church, ¶366). See Immortality.

    Core concept · home chapter

    Divine Inspiration

    Definition. The claim that a creative, prophetic, or moral insight arises from a source beyond the individual's ordinary cognition (God, the Spirit, a muse), and the experienced sense of being “given” an idea.

    ✦ Faith

    Scripture is called theopneustos, “God-breathed” (2 Tim 3:16; theopneustos). Prophecy is described as coming not by human will but from God (2 Pet 1:20–21).

    Bezalel is filled with the Spirit of God for craftsmanship and art (Exod 31:1–5), the Bible's clearest picture of artistic inspiration.

    Other traditions describe a revealed or given source in their own terms, from the Qur'anic idea of revelation to the Vedic idea of what was “heard,” and the spontaneity prized in the Zhuangzi.

    ◇ Philosophy

    Philosophers ask what reports of religious experience can justify: SEP: Religious Experience, Mysticism, Creativity, Faith, and Religion and Epistemology.

    ✦ Faith observation and experience

    William James's The Varieties of Religious Experience (1902) (full text at the Internet Archive; overview) gives four marks of mystical states: ineffability, noetic quality, transiency, and passivity.

    ◆ Science Brain studies of meditation, prayer, and mystical-type experience are on Chapter 04. They describe correlates and reported meaning; they cannot show whether the source is divine.

    A culture of life

    Faith that treats every person as a living soul shows itself most sharply in the protection of life. The story of Hans Georg Calmeyer, told on Chapter 02 and on hanscalmeyer.com, is this site's anchor example.
