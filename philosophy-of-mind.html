    The hard problem

    ◇ Philosophy Explaining how the brain processes information, discriminates and reports is one kind of problem. Explaining why any of it is accompanied by experience at all is another, which David Chalmers called the hard problem (Chalmers, “Facing Up to the Problem of Consciousness,” 1995; IEP: The Hard Problem of Consciousness; overview).

    Key topics: SEP: Consciousness, Qualia, Zombies, the Knowledge Argument, and the Unity of Consciousness.

    History In 2023 a 25-year wager between Chalmers and the neuroscientist Christof Koch, over whether the neural basis of consciousness would be found by then, ended with Chalmers declared the winner: “philosopher 1, neuroscientist 0” (Nature news, 2023).

    Physicalism

    ◇ Philosophy Everything that exists is physical, and mental states are physical states or are fixed by them (SEP: Physicalism). Identity theory equates mental states with brain states (SEP: The Mind/Brain Identity Theory); functionalism defines them by the roles they play (SEP: Functionalism).

    Dualism

    ◇ Philosophy Substance dualism holds that the mind is a distinct, non-physical thing; property dualism holds that there is one kind of substance with irreducibly mental properties (SEP: Dualism). The pressing question for both is how the mental can make a difference to the physical (SEP: Mental Causation); epiphenomenalism answers that it does not (SEP: Epiphenomenalism).

    ✦ Faith The belief that each person has an immortal soul is a faith commitment, even where it is defended with dualist arguments.

    Panpsychism and cosmopsychism

    ◇ Philosophy Panpsychism holds that mentality is a fundamental and widespread feature of the world (SEP: Panpsychism, including its section on cosmopsychism; Chalmers, “Panpsychism and Panprotopsychism”). Cosmopsychism starts from the cosmos as a whole and treats individual minds as derived from it (Cosmopsychism).

    Related: Russellian monism and neutral monism, which take the basic nature of matter to be neither simply physical nor simply mental.

    Idealism

    ◇ Philosophy Reality is fundamentally mental (SEP: Idealism). Buddhist Yogācāra offers an Eastern view with idealist features (SEP: Yogācāra).

    Core concept · home chapter

    Conscious Will

    Definition. The experience and exercise of deliberately initiating action for reasons, distinct from (a) the feeling of willing and (b) the brain processes that precede movement.

    ◆ Science

    In Libet's experiments a readiness potential in the brain began before people reported awareness of the intention to move; later work reinterprets that signal as spontaneous neural fluctuation rather than a hidden decision, and shows that the felt experience of will can be manipulated. The studies, and their limits, are on Chapter 04.

    ◇ Philosophy

    Libertarian (agent-causal), compatibilist, and hard determinist accounts disagree about whether and how we act freely: SEP: Free Will; Compatibilism; Incompatibilism; Causal Determinism; Moral Responsibility.

    ✦ Faith

    Christian theology joins human will to grace: Augustine on will and grace (Confessions); God works in you “to will and to work” (Phil 2:12–13); Aquinas treats the will as rational appetite within his account of the soul (Summa Theologiae, Prima Pars).

    Life Saver · conscience against law

    Conscience versus positive law, and the heroic rescuers

    The historical record. Appointed in March 1941 to adjudicate “doubtful cases” of Jewish ancestry in the occupied Netherlands, the German lawyer Dr. Hans Georg Calmeyer engineered a legal loophole permitting counter-proof of descent. Yad Vashem records that about 60% of applications were approved, most on clearly fictitious documentation, saving at least 3,000 Jewish lives, while secretaries warned rejected petitioners before the German police arrived (Yad Vashem, Righteous Among the Nations; USHMM oral history of Jacob van Proosdij).

    ◇ Philosophy Working inside the Nazi administrative machinery meant that some petitions were rejected. Real-world conscious will is rarely clean or painless; what it shows here is sustained moral agency under extreme constraint and personal risk.

    ◇ Philosophy A core principle from hanscalmeyer.com: “Just because something is legal does not make it right to do.” See SEP: Conscience and SEP: Altruism.

    ◆ Science Samuel and Pearl Oliner’s case-control study of Holocaust rescuers and matched non-rescuers (The Altruistic Personality, 1988; summarized in this MIDUS paper) identified “extensivity,” an expanded sense of attachment and moral responsibility toward strangers and outsiders, as the key distinguishing trait. A formal religious label alone did not sharply separate rescuers from bystanders; what mattered was whether conviction became inclusive obligation toward the vulnerable. The findings rest on retrospective self-reports.
