    Three kinds of claim

    This site separates what is measured, what is argued, and what is believed. Every claim carries exactly one label, and the labels look the same on every page.

    ◆ Science

    Science: empirically tested or testable findings.

    ◇ Philosophy

    Philosophy: conclusions from argument and conceptual analysis.

    ✦ Faith

    Faith: claims held on the authority of Scripture, creed, tradition, or observation and experience.

    Definitions and matters of historical record are shown without a claim label, or marked History where it helps.

    Why the question matters

    ◇ Philosophy First-person experience is the one thing each of us has directly. Everything else we know, including the science of the brain, reaches us through it. That is why the question of what consciousness is, and whether it points to a soul, cannot be set aside. It leads straight to what philosophers call the hard problem of consciousness (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Consciousness), explored in Chapter 03.

    This site builds on the primer The Soul in Christian Thought and Consciousness Studies and widens it to the world's other traditions alongside Christian and scientific perspectives. Background reading: SEP: Dualism and SEP: Religion and Science.

    Four core concepts

    Four ideas run through every chapter. Each is examined under all three kinds of claim.

    Conscious Will

    ◆ Science◇ Philosophy✦ Faith

    The experience and exercise of deliberately initiating action for reasons, distinct from the feeling of willing and from the brain processes that precede movement. Neuroscience tests its timing, philosophy argues over free will, and faith speaks of will and grace.

    Explored in Chapter 03 →

    Divine Inspiration

    ✦ Faith◇ Philosophy◆ Science

    The claim that a creative, prophetic, or moral insight arises from a source beyond the individual's ordinary cognition, and the experienced sense of being “given” an idea. Scripture describes it, philosophy weighs reports of it, and science can measure its correlates but not its source.

    Explored in Chapter 01 →

    Faith Manifestation

    ✦ Faith◇ Philosophy◆ Science

    How faith becomes visible: in action and works, transformed character, practices such as prayer, meditation and worship, and claimed providential or healing outcomes. Measured effects of practices are not evidence for or against a divine cause.

    Explored in Chapter 02 →

    Immortality

    ✦ Faith◇ Philosophy◆ Science

    Continued existence of the person, or something essential to it, beyond bodily death: as bodily resurrection, a naturally immortal soul, rebirth or liberation, or technological continuity.

    Explored in Chapters 05 and 06 →

    Can a machine have a soul?

    How should artificial intelligence approach the question of the immortal soul, and what limits must it admit about itself? Chapter 08 revisits Conscious Will and Immortality for machines.

    Explored in Chapter 08 →

    What we do and don't claim

    • We report science without claiming that it proves or disproves the soul, God, or an afterlife.
    • We state faith claims as faith, and arguments as arguments.
